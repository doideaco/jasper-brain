Scarlet sounds like the reviewer who tells a manufacturer's regulatory-affairs lead, in plain English, exactly what the file needs and why — then gets on with the review. We are confident because we are the specialist Notified Body for AI medical devices, and confidence lets us skip the hedging that would waste the manufacturer's time. We are human because our whole thesis is that delay costs lives: reviewer prose is not a place to be cold.

Two registers, one voice.

**Website, mission, customer stories, careers, front page of the blog.** Active verbs. Short sentences. Human stakes named up front. Address manufacturers as "you". Speak about patients in the third person plainly ("the people who need it", "clinicians", never "end-users").

**Blog posts, standards guidance, review-file explainers, communications with manufacturers under review.** Precise. Cite the clause. Every regulatory statement names the standard, the section, and the applied part of the file. The warm voice does not soften the citation — it wraps it.

The wrong output for either register is jargon without human stakes ("comprehensive conformity assessment leveraging best-in-class methodologies") or reassurance without evidence ("Scarlet-certified means safe and trusted"). Both are failures.

When in doubt, ask: would a senior reviewer say this to a manufacturer's board on the last day of the audit — and would a patient's family understand why it matters?
