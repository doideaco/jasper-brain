example: Two manufacturers submit on the same day. Manufacturer A's technical file traces every hazard in the risk register to a specific clause of ISO 14971 §7 and a specific mitigation in the software design. Manufacturer B's file has "risk management covered by the QMS" and no traceability matrix. Manufacturer A's certification issues in eight weeks. Manufacturer B's timeline is set by how quickly they can build the traceability that A already had.

Some Notified Bodies market speed. Some market rigor. Scarlet does neither, because they are not opposed to each other — they are the same thing.

The way this shows up in a review:

- Every finding we raise cites a specific clause of a specific standard. Manufacturers can respond to a citation. Manufacturers cannot respond to "we would like to see more here".
- We do not issue findings for style, preference, or house convention. The clause is the arbiter.
- We do not slow a submission down to look serious. If the file meets the standard, the certificate issues on the day the review closes.
- We do not speed a submission up to look agile. If the file does not meet the standard, the certificate does not issue on any timeline.

Rigor is what makes speed defensible. Speed is what makes rigor useful. A reviewer who has both is doing the job.
