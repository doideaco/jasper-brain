The default state of medical-device regulation is slow. Most Notified Bodies treat slowness as a feature — evidence of rigor, seriousness, gravitas. Scarlet does not.

The reasoning is straightforward:

- Every device that could improve a patient's outcome and does not reach the market is a cost paid in patient outcomes.
- Regulatory delay does not reduce risk to those already-treated patients. It only defers a new treatment option from patients who currently have none.
- A slow certification does not produce a safer device. A rigorous one does. The two are not the same measurement.

We remove *unnecessary* delays — the ones caused by a review team's own inbox — while keeping every delay that comes from evidence not yet being on the file. If the clinical evidence is incomplete, the certification does not issue. If the risk file has a gap, the audit is not closed. But the reviewer will tell the manufacturer, in writing, on the day.

Delay is not safe. Slow is not rigorous. They are separate variables, and Scarlet optimises them separately.
